Region chart

The Graveyard, drawn as one connected chart

Contributed by @nepure · Covers the Graveyard region on the normal route

How to read the chart

The chart splits the region into two boxes. The upper box is the surface area and the lower box is the underground area. Coloured lines join the map edges that actually connect, so a line leaving one map on its right side and entering another on the left is a single doorway rather than two separate routes.

The annotations are written in Japanese. The upper box is labelled the surface area and the lower box the underground area, the arrow on the surface marks the way through to Korea, and the arrow underground marks the way through to London. BOSS marks the arena at the top of the surface box.

Where it leads

The hand-drawn symbols are the author's own shorthand for the places worth remembering on a run. Each one sits on the map where that landmark is found.

Two of the three regions that close out the route are reachable from this chart: the surface exit leads to Korea and the underground exit leads to London. The third door, into Monochrome, opens from the same grounds and is covered by the official atlas.

Region chart

Monochrome, drawn as one connected chart

Contributed by a player · Covers the Monochrome region on the normal route

How to read the chart

Every map is drawn as the black-and-white pattern you actually see when you walk it, so you can match a tile to where you are standing without reading a single label. Coloured lines join the map edges that connect, and the three coloured boxes group the region into the stretches you clear together.

The annotations are written in Japanese. The brown box at the bottom is the starting area, the orange box above it is the first boss area, and BOSS marks each arena. Read the chart from the bottom upward: the run begins in the starting area and works toward the arena at the top.

Where it leads

Monochrome is the last of the three regions the Graveyard opens into, and it carries three boss arenas rather than one. Two of them sit at the ends of their own branches, so the chart is the quickest way to see which way a branch turns before you commit to walking it.

The hand-drawn patterns are the author's own shorthand. They are recognisable on sight but not exact, so use the official atlas when a zone or a boss name has to be right.
